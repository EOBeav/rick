Camera Overview

Released in April 1984, the Canon T70 was the second model in Canon's innovative T-series lineup. Designed by industrial designer Luigi Colani, its starkly functional, leatherless, leverless body introduced a futuristic "VHS recorder" aesthetic.

Powered by an onboard 8-bit custom microprocessor, the T70 streamlined film loading, winding, and rewinding with built-in motorized automation, bridging the gap between beginner cameras like the T50 and professional flagships like the T90.

Basic Operations Guide

Learn how to setup, shoot in Shutter Priority, and configure Manual mode on your T70.

Step 1 Power & Batteries

The T70 requires two standard AA alkaline batteries (accessible via the side grip door). Slide the main switch up from "LOCK" to "AVERAGE" to power on the camera. Use the BC (battery check) button to verify power levels on the LCD screen.

Step 2 Shutter Priority Mode (Tv)

  1. Set your FD lens aperture ring to the green A (Auto) mark.
  2. Press and hold the MODE button while tapping the ▲ or ▼ shift buttons until Tv appears on the LCD panel.
  3. Select your desired shutter speed using the shift buttons (2s to 1/1000s).
  4. Half-press the shutter button to view the calculated aperture in the viewfinder, then fully press to shoot.

Step 3 Manual Mode (M)

  1. Ensure the camera body is set to Tv mode.
  2. Rotate your lens aperture ring away from the green A to any specific f-stop (e.g., f/4 or f/8). An M icon will flash on the LCD and viewfinder.
  3. Set your preferred shutter speed using the ▲ / ▼ buttons.
  4. Half-press the shutter button to read the recommended aperture in the viewfinder, match your lens ring to it (or offset for exposure control), and take the shot.

Canon T-Series vs. Earlier A-Series

How the computer-driven T70 body differs structurally and functionally from legendary late-1970s A-series cameras like the AE-1.

Canon T-Series (T70)

  • ✓ Power Source: Standard AA batteries (easy to find anywhere).
  • ✓ Film Transport: Built-in motorized automatic loading, winding, and rewinding.
  • ✓ Body Design: Ergonomic, leverless, crankless plastic composite body with digital LCD panels.
  • ✓ Reliability: Improved foam light seal longevity and sturdy battery doors.

Earlier A-Series (AE-1 / A-1)

  • ⚡ Power Source: Expensive, harder-to-find silver-oxide 4LR44/PX28 batteries.
  • ⚡ Film Transport: Manual film advance lever and manual rewind crank (motor winder optional).
  • ⚡ Body Design: Classic metal-top mechanical aesthetic with traditional analog dials and winding levers.
  • ⚡ Common Issues: Prone to degraded light seals ("sticky shutter" / AE-1 squeak) and fragile battery doors.

Used Market Pricing & Buying Guide

Because the T70 was heavily produced and eventually overshadowed by autofocus cameras, it remains one of the best budget values in vintage film photography.

Reputable Used Camera Stores $40 – $80

Purchasing from verified camera dealers usually includes tested shutter speeds, clean electronics, a functional battery door, and a store warranty or return window. Often bundled with the standard FD 50mm f/1.8 lens.

Private Sales & Marketplaces $20 – $40

Finding one on eBay, garage sales, or local classifieds can yield incredible bargains ($10–$20 if you are patient). However, private sales are typically "as-is," so always verify that the battery compartment is clean and the LCD screen powers up.

Collector Tip: Keep an eye out for the rare US Navy-engraved version or bundle deals that include extra FD lenses or the Command Back 70 accessory for time-lapse and data stamping!
