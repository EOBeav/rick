Putting It Into Practice

一期一会 Ichigo Ichie

"One time, one meeting." As you integrate these habits, remember the ethics of radical presence. The light, the subject, and your specific mindset in this exact moment will never occur again.

When you raise your camera, take one deep breath. Treat every scene as a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity. Quit the metrics of social media, and connect with the reality of the present moment.

幽玄 Yugen

The beauty of what stays partly hidden. Don't feel the need to show the viewer everything. A photograph crammed full of information leaves the viewer with nothing to do.

Embrace the mist, the shadows, and the unseen. Give up the "big postcard view" in exchange for the textures and depths that require the viewer's imagination to complete the picture.

The One-Day-At-A-Time Approach

The temptation with a list like this is to try everything at once—and that's exactly what Japanese minimalism cautions against. Pick one concept that resonates with you today. Sit with it for a week. Notice where it shows up in your workflow. Real change accumulates slowly.
