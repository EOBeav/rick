Emulsion Profile

Fine-Grained Modernism in Monochrome

Introduced in 1990, Ilford Delta 400 Professional was engineered using peak 1990s photochemistry—featuring Ilford's Core-Shell Core Technology and tabular (T-Grain) silver halide crystals. Unlike traditional cubic films (such as HP5 Plus or Tri-X), Delta 400 produces flat-lying crystals that maximize light absorption without increasing grain size. This yields ultra-smooth tonality, high edge sharpness, crisp resolution, and a clean, flat-contrast base ideal for digital scanning or custom darkroom enlargement.

Nominal Speed ISO 400/27°

Dynamic Range ~10 Stops

GUIDE Best Practices for Maximum Success

1. Metering & Exposure Strategy

Delta 400’s characteristic curve has a sharper toe than classic films. Under-exposure leads to murky, grainy shadows quickly. Err on the side of over-exposure (e.g., exposing at EI 320) to ensure full shadow density. Highlights are broad and forgiving, allowing rich recovery.

2. Optical Contrast Control

Because Delta 400 produces a relatively flat baseline image designed for post-processing/scanning, use high-contrast lenses or pair the film with a Yellow (Y2) or Orange filter in daylight to boost mid-tone contrast optically while maintaining smooth grain.

3. Agitation & Stand Development Warning

If semi-stand developing (e.g., using Caffenol or Rodinal), Delta 400 requires more frequent or aggressive inversions than standard cubic films to avoid bromide drag/streaking due to the unique emulsion density and chemistry interaction.

ISO Rating & Pushing Performance

Delta 400 is fine-tuned for ISO 400, but can be rated across different speeds depending on light availability and contrast goals.

ISO 400 Box Speed

Silky-smooth tones, nearly invisible grain in 35mm, crisp edge definition, and maximum dynamic range (~10 stops). Yields a slightly flatter image ready for scanning or darkroom interpretation.

Best For: Studio portraits, bright day shooting, fine detail.
ISO 800 +1 Stop Push

Increases mid-tone contrast slightly without sacrificing significant detail. Grain structure begins to show slightly more texture (resembling standard HP5+ at box speed).

Best For: Overcast days, street photography, added punch.
ISO 1600 +2 Stops Push

Denser shadows and prominent, tighter grain structure. Contrast increases noticeably. Requires extended development in speed-enhancing developers (e.g., Ilfotec DD-X or Microphen).

Best For: Low-light indoor action, high-contrast imagery.
ISO 3200 +3 Stops Push

Extreme push limit. High contrast with deep, clipped blacks and distinct grain. While achievable in specialized developers, Ilford Delta 3200 is generally recommended for true ISO 3200 needs.

Best For: Night shooting, low light situations.
